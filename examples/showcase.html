Content integrity

The alarming rise of AI slop

Slop is not a taste complaint. It is a measurable distribution of five lexical markers across published text, and over eight quarters that distribution has stopped looking like an artefact and started looking like the baseline.

Eight quarters ago the markers appeared in 4 percent of the documents we index. They now appear in 57 percent. The part that matters is not the publishing figure but the corpus figure trailing one quarter behind it: the crawler cannot distinguish generated text from the rest, so 48 percent of what the next generation trains on already carries the markers it will go on to reproduce. Nobody decided this. Every individual document was defensible.

What this does not cover

The detector counts markers. It does not read for truth, usefulness, or whether a human wrote the thing. A careful writer who genuinely likes the word delve scores as slop, and a fabricated paragraph in plain words scores clean.

It is calibrated on English prose only, and says nothing about code, images, or translated text. A high score is a reason to read a document, not a verdict on it.
